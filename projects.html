Put each part of the course to work on a real dataset, step by step, with automatic checks.

Four guided projects on real datasets — one at the end of each part from Part 2 on. Each comes as two Jupyter notebooks: a starter where you write the key lines (marked # TODO, with an automatic check after every step) and a complete solution with all outputs. The datasets ship with scikit-learn, so nothing needs downloading.

Part 2 · Learning from labelled data

Predicting diabetes progression

Predict disease progression from the baseline measurements, and find out how much better than a naive guess you can do.

The data. 442 diabetes patients from the study by Efron, Hastie, Johnstone and Tibshirani (2004), shipped with scikit-learn as load_diabetes. Ten measurements per patient — age, sex, body-mass index (BMI), blood pressure and six blood-serum values (s1–s6) — and a number measuring how far the disease progressed one year later.

You will practise

  • Train/test split and a baseline
  • Simple and multiple linear regression
  • MAE and R²
  • Ridge regression with the penalty chosen by cross-validation
  • Reading (and distrusting) coefficients

Uses: Lesson 5 · Lesson 6 · Lesson 9 · Lesson 10

Steps

  1. Load and look at the data
  2. Split the data and set a baseline
  3. One feature: BMI
  4. All ten features
  5. Don't over-read the coefficients
  6. Ridge, with the penalty chosen by cross-validation
  7. Compare fairly with cross-validation

Go further: Add squared terms with PolynomialFeatures(degree=2) in the pipeline. Does cross-validated MAE improve? Try LassoCV: which features does it switch off? Plot predicted against actual progression for the test set. Where are the biggest errors?

Part 3 · Classic algorithms

Classifying breast tumours

Build a classifier that flags malignant tumours, choose between five algorithms fairly, and tune it so that it almost never misses a malignant case.

The data. 569 breast-tumour biopsies from the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer study (Street, Wolberg and Mangasarian, 1993), shipped with scikit-learn as load_breast_cancer. Each has 30 measurements of the cell nuclei in a digitised image (radius, texture, smoothness, …) and a diagnosis: malignant or benign.

You will practise

  • Stratified train/test split
  • Comparing logistic regression, kNN, a decision tree, a random forest and an SVM with cross-validation
  • Confusion matrix, precision and recall
  • Moving the decision threshold
  • Feature importance

Uses: Lesson 8 · Lesson 9 · Lesson 11 · Lesson 13 · Lesson 14 · Lesson 15

Steps

  1. Load the data
  2. Split, keeping the class balance
  3. Compare five algorithms with cross-validation
  4. Test the winner once
  5. Never miss a malignant tumour: lower the threshold
  6. Which measurements matter?
What the solution finds (open after you've tried)
Best by 5-fold CVlogistic regression (0.981)
Test set, threshold 0.5precision 1.00 · recall 0.96
Threshold 0.1recall 1.00 — no malignant tumour missed
Most important featuremean concave points

Go further: Tune C for logistic regression with GridSearchCV. Draw the ROC curve with RocCurveDisplay.from_estimator and read off the AUC. Which threshold would you choose, and how would you explain the trade-off to a doctor?

Part 4 · Finding structure without labels

Discovering wine cultivars

Pretend we don't know the cultivars. Can PCA and clustering rediscover the three groups from the chemistry alone?

The data. 178 wines from three grape cultivars grown in the same region of Italy (Forina et al.), shipped with scikit-learn as load_wine. Thirteen chemical measurements per wine: alcohol, malic acid, flavanoids, colour intensity, proline and more. The cultivar labels exist — but we hide them until the very end.

You will practise

  • Standardising before PCA and k-means
  • Explained variance
  • Choosing k with the elbow and silhouette scores
  • Checking clusters against hidden labels (adjusted Rand index)

Uses: Lesson 16 · Lesson 17

Steps

  1. Load the chemistry (and put the labels aside)
  2. Standardise
  3. PCA: how many directions matter?
  4. Look at the data in 2D
  5. Choose k
  6. Reveal the labels
What the solution finds (open after you've tried)
PC1 + PC2 explain55% of the variance

Go further: Repeat the clustering on the first two PCA components only. Is the ARI better or worse? Try agglomerative clustering and draw a dendrogram with SciPy. What happens to the silhouette scores if you forget to standardise?

Part 5 · Neural networks & real projects

Recognising handwritten digits

Train a neural network to read the digits, compare it with simpler models, and study the mistakes it makes.

The data. 1,797 handwritten digits (0–9) from the UCI optical recognition dataset (Alpaydin and Kaynak), shipped with scikit-learn as load_digits. Each image is 8×8 pixels with brightness 0–16, flattened into 64 numbers.

You will practise

  • Images as feature vectors
  • Training an MLP (multilayer perceptron)
  • Honest model comparison
  • Multi-class confusion matrices
  • Looking at errors

Uses: Lesson 18 · Lesson 19 · Lesson 20

Steps

  1. Load and look at some digits
  2. Split
  3. A baseline: logistic regression
  4. A neural network
  5. Is the neural network really best?
  6. Study the mistakes
