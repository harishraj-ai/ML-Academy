Going deeper: PageRank as an eigenvector, and the graph Laplacian

Intermediate · skip on a first read

The linear algebra behind two of this lesson's methods: why PageRank is an eigenvector and why repeated multiplication finds it, and how the eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian split a graph into communities.

You'll need: this lesson and eigenvectors from Lesson 3's "Going deeper".

PageRank is an eigenvector

The random surfer's moves form a matrix $G = \frac{1-d}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top + d M$, where $M$ follows links. PageRank is the vector $r$ with $r G = r$: an eigenvector with eigenvalue 1. Because every entry of $G$ is positive, the Perron–Frobenius theorem guarantees this eigenvalue is the largest and the eigenvector is unique and positive, which is why power iteration (multiplying by $G$ again and again) converges to it. The largest eigenvalue is exactly 1, power iteration converges in 27 steps, and its answer matches the eigenvector to $10^{-11}$. The damping factor sets the speed: errors shrink by a factor of about $d$ each step. On this directed graph, PageRank correlates with in-degree (0.724), not out-degree (−0.208): links to a node count, links from it don't.

The graph Laplacian

The Laplacian is $L = D - A$, where $D$ holds the degrees on its diagonal. For any vector $f$, $f^\top L f = \sum_{\text{edges } ij}(f_i - f_j)^2$: small when connected nodes get similar values. Its smallest eigenvalue is always 0 (a constant vector), and the eigenvector of the second-smallest, the Fiedler vector, is the smoothest non-constant way to give nodes values, which tends to split the graph along its weakest cut:

The eigenvalues start at 0, then 2.091; splitting the nodes by the sign of the Fiedler vector recovers the two planted communities exactly. Spectral clustering does this with several eigenvectors and k-means. The same Laplacian appears in label spreading (Lesson 32) and in graph neural networks, where a layer smooths node features over neighbours.
