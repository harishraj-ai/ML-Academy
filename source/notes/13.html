Part 3 · Lesson 13

Decision trees

1Twenty questions with data

Find an object with yes/no questions ("Is it alive?", "Bigger than a cat?"). A tree classifies the same way: "Is petal width ≤ 0.8 cm?" … until it reaches an answer. A single tree is easy to explain; combined into forests and boosting (Lesson 14), trees are often the strongest models for tabular data.

2Anatomy of a tree

  • Root: all the data, the first question.
  • Internal node: one feature vs a threshold, "$x_j\le t$?" (yes → left, no → right).
  • Leaf: the prediction (majority class, or average for regression).
  • Depth: the number of questions on the longest path.
Example
Petal width 1.2: is 1.2 ≤ 0.8? No → right. Is 1.2 ≤ 1.75? Yes → versicolor. Anyone can follow that.

Each split cuts the feature space with an axis-parallel line, so the regions are boxes.

3Choosing questions: impurity

Sorting laundry
A pile of only white shirts is "pure": you know what to do. A mixed pile is "impure". A tree keeps asking until its piles are as pure as possible.

Gini $G=1-\sum_kp_k^2\qquad$ Entropy $H=-\sum_kp_k\log_2p_k$

Both are 0 for a pure node and largest for an even mix.

Example
6 yes / 4 no → Gini $1-(0.36+0.16)=$ 0.48. 5 / 5 → 0.5 (the maximum for two classes).

4Scoring a split: information gain

$G_{\text{split}}=\frac{n_L}{n}G(\text{left})+\frac{n_R}{n}G(\text{right}),\qquad\text{Gain}=G(\text{parent})-G_{\text{split}}$

Example from the lesson
Parent 6 yes / 4 no (Gini 0.48).
Split A: left 4/0 (pure, 0), right 2/4 (0.444) → weighted 0.267 → gain 0.213.
Split B: both sides 3/2, the same mix as the parent → gain 0. A is much better.

5Growing it: CART

  1. At a node, try every feature and every threshold (midpoints between sorted values).
  2. Pick the split with the biggest impurity decrease.
  3. Split, then repeat on each child until a stopping rule (pure, max depth, too few examples).

Greedy: the best split right now, no looking ahead. Not guaranteed optimal (finding the best tree is far too hard), but it works very well.

6The demo

Raise the maximum depth and the tree adds axis-parallel cuts: underfits at low depth, carves tiny boxes around single points at high depth (overfits).

7Regression trees

A leaf predicts the average $y$ of its examples; impurity = the variance (MSE) in the node. The result is a step function: flat inside each box, jumps between boxes.

8Trees overfit easily

Left alone, a tree splits until every leaf is pure and memorises the noise. Controls (pick by CV):

  • max_depth: fewer questions.
  • min_samples_split / min_samples_leaf: no tiny groups.
  • max_leaf_nodes: cap the leaves.
  • Cost-complexity pruning (ccp_alpha): grow fully, then cut back, minimising $R_\alpha(T)=R(T)+\alpha|T|$.

9Feature importance

Add up each feature's impurity reductions (weighted by examples), normalise to 1. It shows what the tree relied on.

Favours features with many distinct values (IDs, continuous numbers). With correlated features the tree may use one and make the other look useless. It describes the model, not causation.

10Strengths and weaknesses

+ easy to read
+ no scaling (splits compare one feature with a threshold)
+ numeric and categorical, non-linear, interactions
+ fast
− overfit without limits
− unstable: small data change → very different tree
− staircase boundaries (a diagonal needs many steps)
− rarely the most accurate alone

The fix for instability: many trees combined (Lesson 14).

★Going deeper: pruning, honest importances and instability

Cost-complexity pruning

Grow a hedge freely, then trim it back. Every leaf has a "price", and you keep a branch only if it fixes enough mistakes to pay for its leaves. Raise the price and more branches get cut.

Keep or cut this branch?
A branch adds 3 leaves and reduces the training error rate by 0.06.
At $\alpha=0.01$: cost $3\times0.01=0.03$; saving 0.06 > 0.03 → keep.
At $\alpha=0.03$: cost 0.09 > 0.06 → cut.
As $\alpha$ rises, the weakest branches go first ("weakest-link" pruning), giving a sequence of smaller and smaller trees.

Feature importance: impurity vs permutation

To find out how much a football team depends on a player, replace them with a random stranger and watch what happens to the score. Permutation importance does this for features: shuffle one column and see how much worse the model gets on new data.

Reading it
Impurity importance ranks the noise column 6th of 31, ahead of 25 real medical measurements.
Permutation importance on test data: exactly 0. Shuffling noise changes nothing.

Trees are unstable

In a guessing game, the first question decides which questions come after. Change a few people in the room and a different first question may look best, and then every later question changes too. A tree is built the same way, one greedy split at a time.

In one breath
Grow the tree fully and prune it with a price per leaf chosen by cross-validation; trust permutation importance on test data, not impurity importance; and remember a small change in the data can reshape a whole tree, which is why forests average many.
✎ in one line: ask the question that makes the purest groups, repeat, prune before it memorises, and trust permutation importance.
