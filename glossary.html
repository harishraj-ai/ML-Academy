Glossary

182 terms in plain English. Each one links to the lesson section where it's explained in full.

ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUVW

A

Accuracy
Fraction of predictions that are correct: (TP + TN) / total. Misleading on imbalanced data. Lesson 9 →
Activation function
The non-linear function applied after a neuron's weighted sum, e.g. ReLU, sigmoid, tanh, softmax. Lesson 18 →
Active learning
Choosing which examples to label next, to learn most from each label. Lesson 32 →
AdaBoost
Boosting method that re-weights training examples so each new weak learner focuses on previous mistakes. Lesson 14 →
Adam
A gradient-descent optimiser that combines momentum with a per-parameter adaptive step size. Lesson 19 →
Adjacency matrix
A matrix with a 1 where two nodes are connected and 0 otherwise. Lesson 34 →
Attention
Mechanism where each token computes a softmax-weighted average of other tokens' values, using query–key similarity. Lesson 19 →
AUC (ROC-AUC)
Area under the ROC curve: the probability a random positive is scored above a random negative. 0.5 = random, 1 = perfect. Lesson 9 →
Average treatment effect (ATE)
The average over a population of the difference between outcomes with and without treatment. Lesson 29 →

B

Backpropagation
Efficient computation of all gradients in a neural network by applying the chain rule backwards from the output. Lesson 18 →
Backtesting
Evaluating forecasts by repeatedly training up to a date and forecasting the next block (rolling origin). Lesson 26 →
Bagging
Bootstrap aggregating: train models on bootstrap samples and average their predictions to reduce variance. Lesson 14 →
Batch / mini-batch
The group of examples used to compute one gradient update. Lesson 7 →
Bayes' theorem
P(A | B) = P(B | A) P(A) / P(B): updates a prior belief with evidence into a posterior. Lesson 4 →
Bayesian network
A directed acyclic graph with a conditional probability table for each node given its parents. Lesson 35 →
Bayesian optimisation
Hyperparameter search that fits a surrogate model of the score and chooses each next setting to balance exploration and exploitation. Lesson 23 →
Bellman equation
The relation that a state's value equals the best expected reward plus discounted value of the next state. Lesson 31 →
Bias (error)
Error from wrong assumptions; high bias causes underfitting. Lesson 10 →
Bias (parameter)
The intercept term b added to a weighted sum. Lesson 6 →
Bootstrap sample
A sample of n examples drawn with replacement from n originals (about 63% distinct). Lesson 14 →

C

Canary deployment
Sending a small share of traffic to a new model and increasing it if it performs well. Lesson 30 →
Censoring
When an event hasn't been observed by the end of follow-up, so only a lower bound on its time is known. Lesson 33 →
Centroid
The mean point of a cluster in k-means. Lesson 16 →
Chain rule
Derivative of a composition: d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x)) g′(x). Lesson 3 →
Class imbalance
When one class is far rarer than the others, so accuracy becomes misleading. Lesson 25 →
Classification
Supervised learning where the label is a category. Lesson 1 →
Clustering
Grouping unlabelled examples by similarity. Lesson 16 →
Cold start
The problem of recommending for new users or items with no interaction history. Lesson 27 →
Collaborative filtering
Recommending from the interactions of many users: similar users like similar items. Lesson 27 →
Concept drift
A change in the relationship between inputs and the outcome, which lowers performance. Lesson 30 →
Concordance index
The share of comparable pairs a survival model orders correctly; 0.5 is random. Lesson 33 →
Confounder
A variable that affects both the treatment and the outcome, biasing naive comparisons. Lesson 29 →
Confusion matrix
Table of TP, FP, FN and TN counts (or a K×K table for K classes). Lesson 9 →
Conjugate prior
A prior whose posterior has the same family, such as Beta for a probability, so updating is simple arithmetic. Lesson 28 →
Convolution
Sliding a small kernel over an input and summing element-wise products at each position. Lesson 19 →
Covariance
Average product of two features' deviations from their means; positive if they rise together. Lesson 17 →
Cox proportional hazards model
A survival regression where features multiply a baseline hazard by e^(βx). Lesson 33 →
Credible interval
An interval containing a parameter with a stated probability under the posterior. Lesson 28 →
Cross-validation (k-fold)
Split training data into k folds; train on k−1 and validate on the remaining one, k times; average the scores. Lesson 5 →
Curse of dimensionality
In many dimensions data becomes sparse and distances lose meaning. Lesson 11 →

D

Data drift
A change in the distribution of a model's inputs compared with its training data. Lesson 30 →
Data leakage
Information from validation/test data (or the future) sneaking into training, inflating scores. Lesson 5 →
DBSCAN
Density-based clustering that grows clusters from core points and labels isolated points as noise. Lesson 16 →
Decision boundary
The surface where a classifier switches its prediction from one class to another. Lesson 8 →
Decision tree
Model that predicts by asking a sequence of feature-threshold questions. Lesson 13 →
Demographic parity
Fairness criterion: every group is selected (approved, flagged) at the same rate. Summarised by the ratio of the lower to the higher selection rate. Lesson 21 →
Derivative
Slope of a function at a point; how fast the output changes as the input changes. Lesson 3 →
Difference-in-differences
An effect estimate: the treated group's change over time minus a comparable control group's change. Lesson 29 →
Dimensionality reduction
Compressing many features into fewer while keeping most information. Lesson 17 →
Dot product
Sum of products of matching components: a·b = Σ aᵢbᵢ. Lesson 2 →
Dropout
Randomly switching off neurons during training to reduce overfitting. Lesson 19 →

E

Eigenvector / eigenvalue
A direction v that a matrix only stretches: Σv = λv; λ is the stretch factor. Lesson 17 →
Ensemble
A combination of several models whose predictions are pooled. Lesson 14 →
Entropy
Impurity measure −Σ p log₂ p; 0 for a pure node. Lesson 13 →
Epoch
One full pass over the training data. Lesson 7 →
Equal opportunity
Fairness criterion: the true-positive rate is the same in every group, so qualified people are approved equally often. Lesson 21 →
Equalised odds
Fairness criterion: both the true-positive rate and the false-positive rate are equal across groups. Lesson 21 →
Explaining away
When observing a shared effect makes two independent causes dependent. Lesson 35 →

F

F1 score
Harmonic mean of precision and recall: 2TP / (2TP + FP + FN). Lesson 9 →
Feature
An input variable describing an example (x). Lesson 1 →
Feature engineering
Creating and transforming features to make patterns easier to learn. Lesson 20 →
Foreign key
A column that refers to the primary key of another table, linking the two. Lesson 22 →

G

Gaussian process
A prior over functions defined by a kernel, giving a predictive mean and uncertainty at every input. Lesson 28 →
Generalization
How well a model performs on new, unseen data. Lesson 5 →
Gini impurity
Impurity measure 1 − Σ p²; 0 for a pure node. Lesson 13 →
Gradient
Vector of all partial derivatives; points in the direction of steepest increase. Lesson 3 →
Gradient boosting
Boosting that fits each new tree to the residuals (negative gradient) of the current ensemble. Lesson 14 →
Gradient descent
Optimisation by repeatedly stepping against the gradient: w ← w − η∇L. Lesson 7 →

H

Hazard
The instantaneous event rate at a time among those still at risk. Lesson 33 →
Hidden Markov model (HMM)
A model of hidden states following a Markov chain, each emitting an observation. Lesson 35 →
Hinge loss
max(0, 1 − y·score); zero for points correctly outside the SVM margin. Lesson 15 →
Homophily
The tendency of connected nodes to be similar. Lesson 34 →
Hyperparameter
A setting chosen before training (k, λ, learning rate, depth), usually by cross-validation. Lesson 10 →
Hyperparameter
A setting chosen before training that controls how a model learns, such as λ, k, tree depth or the learning rate. Lesson 23 →

I

ICE plot
Individual conditional expectation: one partial-dependence curve per row, revealing differences between rows. Lesson 24 →
Implicit feedback
Interactions such as clicks, views or purchases, used as signals of preference instead of explicit ratings. Lesson 27 →
Imputation
Filling in missing values, e.g. with the training median. Lesson 20 →
Inertia
k-means objective: sum of squared distances from points to their nearest centroid. Lesson 16 →
Isolation Forest
An anomaly detector that isolates points with random splits; anomalies need fewer splits. Lesson 25 →

K

k-means
Clustering algorithm alternating 'assign to nearest centroid' and 'move centroid to mean'. Lesson 16 →
k-nearest neighbours
Predicts from the majority (or average) of the k most similar training examples. Lesson 11 →
Kaplan–Meier estimator
A survival-curve estimate multiplying the share surviving each event time among those at risk. Lesson 33 →
Kernel (CNN)
A small learned grid of weights slid over an image; also called a filter. Lesson 19 →
Kernel trick (SVM)
Computing dot products in a high-dimensional feature space directly from the original vectors. Lesson 15 →

L

L1 / Lasso
Penalty λΣ|w|; sets many weights to exactly zero. Lesson 10 →
L2 / Ridge
Penalty λΣw²; shrinks all weights smoothly (weight decay). Lesson 10 →
Label
The target value to predict (y). Lesson 1 →
Label spreading
A graph method that propagates known labels to unlabelled neighbours until they settle. Lesson 32 →
Lag feature
The value of a series a fixed number of steps earlier, used as a feature. Lesson 26 →
Laplace smoothing
Adding 1 to every count so no probability is ever zero. Lesson 12 →
Learning rate
Step size η in gradient descent. Lesson 7 →
LEFT JOIN
A join that keeps every row of the left table and fills the right table's columns with NULL where there is no match. Lesson 22 →
LIME
Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations: fits a simple weighted model around one prediction to explain it. Lesson 24 →
Link prediction
Scoring unconnected pairs of nodes by how likely they are to be connected. Lesson 34 →
Local Outlier Factor (LOF)
An anomaly score comparing a point's local density with its neighbours' densities. Lesson 25 →
Log loss (cross-entropy)
−[y ln p + (1−y) ln(1−p)]; punishes confident wrong probabilities heavily. Lesson 8 →
Logistic regression
Linear classifier that outputs p = σ(w·x + b). Lesson 8 →
Loss function
A number measuring how wrong a model's predictions are; training minimises it. Lesson 1 →
LSTM
Recurrent network with gates and a cell state that remember over long sequences. Lesson 19 →

M

Margin (SVM)
Width of the gap between the classes around the boundary: 2/‖w‖. Lesson 15 →
Markov decision process (MDP)
A model of sequential decisions: states, actions, transition probabilities, rewards and a discount factor. Lesson 31 →
MASE
Mean absolute scaled error: a forecast's MAE divided by the seasonal naive MAE on the training data; below 1 beats the baseline. Lesson 26 →
Matrix
A rectangular table of numbers; a dataset X has one row per example. Lesson 2 →
Matrix factorisation
Approximating the user–item matrix as a product of user and item latent-factor matrices. Lesson 27 →
MCMC
Markov chain Monte Carlo: algorithms such as Metropolis that draw samples from a posterior using only density ratios. Lesson 28 →
Mean squared error (MSE)
Average of squared errors: (1/n) Σ (ŷᵢ − yᵢ)². Lesson 6 →
MLOps
Practices for deploying, monitoring, retraining and maintaining ML models in production. Lesson 30 →
Model card
A short document that records a model's intended use, how it was evaluated (including per group) and its known limitations. Lesson 21 →
Modularity
How many more edges fall inside groups than random wiring with the same degrees would give. Lesson 34 →

N

Naive Bayes
Classifier using Bayes' theorem with features assumed independent given the class. Lesson 12 →
Neural network (MLP)
Layers of neurons, each computing an activation of a weighted sum. Lesson 18 →
Norm
Length of a vector; Euclidean (L2) √Σv², Manhattan (L1) Σ|v|. Lesson 2 →
Normal distribution
The bell curve, set by mean μ and standard deviation σ; 68/95/99.7% within 1/2/3σ. Lesson 4 →
Normal equation
Closed-form least-squares solution w = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy. Lesson 6 →

O

One-hot encoding
Turning a category into 0/1 columns, one per category. Lesson 20 →
One-standard-error rule
Choose the simplest model whose cross-validated score is within one standard error of the best. Lesson 23 →
Overfitting
Fitting noise in the training data: low training error, high test error. Lesson 5 →

P

PageRank
A centrality score in which a node is important if important nodes link to it. Lesson 34 →
Parameter
A number inside the model learned during training (weights, biases). Lesson 1 →
Partial dependence plot (PDP)
A curve of the average prediction as one feature is set to each value in turn for every row. Lesson 24 →
PCA
Principal component analysis: projects data onto the directions of greatest variance. Lesson 17 →
Permutation importance
The drop in a model's held-out score when one feature's values are randomly shuffled. Lesson 24 →
Pipeline
A chain of preprocessing steps and a model, fitted together on training data. Lesson 20 →
Point-in-time correctness
Building every feature only from data that existed before the moment each prediction would have been made, to avoid leakage. Lesson 22 →
Pooling
Downsampling a feature map, e.g. keeping the max of each 2×2 window. Lesson 19 →
Population stability index (PSI)
A drift measure: the sum over bins of (live share − training share) × ln(live ÷ training). Lesson 30 →
Posterior
The updated probability distribution over unknowns after seeing data: likelihood × prior, normalised. Lesson 28 →
Precision
Of predicted positives, the fraction truly positive: TP / (TP + FP). Lesson 9 →
Precision at k
The share of real positives among the k highest-scored cases, used when people review a ranked list. Lesson 25 →
Precision@k
The share of the top k recommendations that are relevant to the user. Lesson 27 →
Primary key
A column (or set of columns) whose value uniquely identifies each row of a table. Lesson 22 →
Prior / posterior
Belief before evidence (prior) and after updating with Bayes' theorem (posterior). Lesson 4 →
Propensity score
The probability of receiving treatment given a person's characteristics. Lesson 29 →
Proxy feature
A feature that stands in for another, often sensitive, attribute because it is correlated with it — e.g. a postcode standing in for ethnicity. Lesson 21 →
Pseudo-label
A model's confident prediction for an unlabelled example, used as if it were a label. Lesson 32 →

Q

Q-learning
A model-free RL algorithm that learns action values from experience with temporal-difference updates. Lesson 31 →

R

Random forest
Bagged decision trees that each consider a random subset of features at every split. Lesson 14 →
Random search
Hyperparameter search that samples settings from distributions; usually more efficient than a grid for the same budget. Lesson 23 →
Recall (sensitivity)
Of actual positives, the fraction found: TP / (TP + FN). Lesson 9 →
Regression
Supervised learning where the label is a number. Lesson 1 →
Regularization
Adding a penalty on model complexity to reduce overfitting. Lesson 10 →
ReLU
Activation max(0, z); default for hidden layers. Lesson 18 →
Residual
The difference between a true value and the prediction. Lesson 6 →
Reward hacking
An agent maximising its reward in a way its designers didn't intend. Lesson 31 →
RMSE / MAE
Root mean squared error (punishes big errors) and mean absolute error. Lesson 9 →
ROC curve
True positive rate vs false positive rate across all thresholds. Lesson 9 →
R² (coefficient of determination)
1 − SS_res / SS_tot: fraction of variance explained compared with predicting the mean. Lesson 6 →

S

Seasonal naive forecast
A baseline forecast that repeats the value from one season earlier, such as the same day last week. Lesson 26 →
Seasonality
A pattern that repeats with a fixed period, such as day of week or month of year. Lesson 26 →
Semi-supervised learning
Learning from a small labelled set together with a large unlabelled set. Lesson 32 →
SHAP
SHapley Additive exPlanations: methods and a library that compute Shapley values for model predictions efficiently. Lesson 24 →
Shapley value
A feature's fair share of a prediction minus the baseline: its average marginal contribution over all orders in which features could be added. Lesson 24 →
Sigmoid
σ(z) = 1/(1 + e^(−z)); squashes any number into (0, 1). Lesson 8 →
Silhouette score
(b − a)/max(a, b) averaged over points; higher means tighter, better-separated clusters. Lesson 16 →
SMOTE
Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique: creates new minority examples on lines between a minority point and its minority neighbours. Lesson 25 →
Softmax
Turns a vector of scores into probabilities summing to 1: e^(zₖ) / Σ e^(zⱼ). Lesson 8 →
Standard deviation
Square root of the variance; typical distance from the mean. Lesson 4 →
Standardisation (z-score)
(x − μ)/σ: rescale a feature to mean 0 and standard deviation 1. Lesson 4 →
Stationary distribution
The long-run share of time a Markov chain spends in each state. Lesson 35 →
Stochastic gradient descent (SGD)
Gradient descent using one random example (or a mini-batch) per update. Lesson 7 →
Successive halving
A search that trains many candidates on a small budget and repeatedly keeps the best fraction with a larger budget. Lesson 23 →
Supervised learning
Learning from examples that have labels. Lesson 1 →
Support vectors
Training points on or inside the SVM margin; they alone determine the boundary. Lesson 15 →
SVM
Support vector machine: a maximum-margin classifier, extended to curved boundaries with kernels. Lesson 15 →

T

t-SNE
Non-linear method for visualising high-dimensional data in 2D; for exploration only. Lesson 17 →
Test set
Data held back until the end for one honest estimate of performance. Lesson 5 →
Thompson sampling
A bandit strategy that chooses each option with the probability that it's the best, by sampling from posteriors. Lesson 28 →
Threshold
Probability cut-off that turns a score into a class decision. Lesson 9 →
Transformer
Architecture built on self-attention; the basis of modern language models. Lesson 19 →

U

Uncertainty sampling
An active-learning strategy that labels the examples the model is least sure about. Lesson 32 →
Underfitting
Model too simple to capture the pattern: both training and test error high. Lesson 5 →
Unsupervised learning
Learning structure from data without labels. Lesson 1 →
Uplift modelling
Estimating each individual's treatment effect, to target an action at those it changes. Lesson 29 →

V

Validation curve
A plot of training and cross-validated scores against one hyperparameter, showing underfitting and overfitting. Lesson 23 →
Validation set
Data used to compare models and choose hyperparameters. Lesson 5 →
Value iteration
Solving an MDP by repeatedly applying the Bellman equation to every state until values converge. Lesson 31 →
Vanishing gradients
Gradients shrinking through many layers so early layers barely learn. Lesson 19 →
Variance (error)
Error from sensitivity to the particular training set; high variance causes overfitting. Lesson 10 →
Variance (statistics)
Average squared distance from the mean. Lesson 4 →
Vector
An ordered list of numbers; e.g. one example's features or a model's weights. Lesson 2 →
Viterbi algorithm
Dynamic programming that finds the most likely sequence of hidden states in an HMM. Lesson 35 →

W

Window function
A SQL function computed over related rows (a window) without collapsing them, written with OVER (PARTITION BY … ORDER BY …). Lesson 22 →
